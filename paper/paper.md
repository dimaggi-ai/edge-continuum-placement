**Margaret Nanyonga**, DIMAGGI AI

## Abstract

The AI-RAN pitch — "an AI factory at every tower" — and the physics of lossless GPU fabrics cannot both be right, because PFC-based lossless Ethernet requires ≈2× the one-way bandwidth–delay product — a full round trip of line-rate data — as per-port headroom (Bifrost's testbed reserved ~9.5 MB for 80 km at 100 G; its simulation needed ~286 MB for 600 km at 400 G) and synchronous training degrades ~26× at 1,000 km in the one published cross-datacenter simulation study. We resolve the tension with an executable placement model: four tier envelopes on the operator's footprint (tower, aggregation hub, metro PoP, central factory), twelve workload archetypes, and a cascade of orthogonal gates — fabric, power, latency, and gravity (data mass and sovereignty) — in which every workload defaults to the most central tier and every edgeward placement must name the gate that forced it. Three findings fall out. **(1) The tower is never the recommended tier**: everything it can host, the aggregation hub hosts better; a modeled national fleet of 30,000 GPU-equipped towers (60,000 GPUs) strands ~87% of its provisioned power in 2-GPU fabric quanta and delivers ~6.4× less schedulable FP8 per provisioned megawatt than 300 hub pods on a third of the power. **(2) Latency is the weakest argument for the edge**: a 100 ms interactive SLA clears a ~500 km path; what actually forces placement below the central factory is the ~100 µs eCPRI fronthaul budget (~20 km radius), backhaul economics of high-rate ingest (~81 TB/month per 0.25 Gbps), and sovereignty — and sovereignty pins a region, not a tower. **(3) The demark discipline**: RDMA stays inside a PoP's latency domain; what crosses the carrier WAN is SRv6/EVPN-carried IP — crossing the demark is RPC, never RDMA. In our synchronization model only the bandwidth term of a ring all-reduce can overlap with compute while the 2(n−1) serial hop latencies stay exposed, which reproduces the published result that doubling inter-site bandwidth moves training efficiency by ≤0.66%: **bandwidth cannot buy back distance**. Where geography strands a radio beyond hub reach, the model honestly reverses — a rural ladder with a 30 km hub makes the tower the recommended tier for RAN L1 — so the tower's job description is an output, not an assumption.

## 1. Introduction

Operators evaluating AI-RAN face a placement question dressed up as a product question: which of the twelve things a converged site could run should run at which of the four rungs an operator actually owns? Vendor material answers with the most distributed rung; cloud material answers with the most central. Neither names the constraint that decides. This paper makes the constraints executable. The contribution is not a new physics result — every constant is sourced — but the *cascade*: a placement engine in which centralization is the null hypothesis, each gate can only force or forbid, and the recommendation must carry the name of its forcing gate. That structure makes the matrix auditable: a reader who disputes a verdict can point at the single gate that produced it, change the envelope, and rerun.

## 2. Model

**Tiers.** Four envelopes: tower (~1.5 kW budget for compute, air-cooled, ~2 accelerators), aggregation hub (~50 kW, liquid-capable, a 64-GPU pod, staffed access), metro PoP (~2 MW, 2,048-GPU fabric), central factory (~100 MW). Each carries a distance-to-user and a transit-RTT rung (0.1/0.3/1.0/10 ms). The envelopes are modeling assumptions, stated in code and overridable.

**Gates.** *Fabric*: a workload demanding more coupled GPUs than the tier's lossless domain can host is blocked; lossless domains end at ~40 km — the reach of the longest InfiniBand product sold, which shipping AI clusters do not use. *Power*: sustained draw and cooling class against the envelope; air cooling caps per-device draw. *Latency*: access RTT + distance RTT (4.9 µs/km fiber, ×2 for round trip) + transit rung against the workload's SLA; the eCPRI fronthaul budget (~100 µs one-way) is a hard variant. *Gravity*: raw ingest above ~0.25 Gbps makes backhaul the dominant cost and forces data-side placement, and a sovereignty pin restricts eligible tiers to the operator's regional footprint. Verdicts are VIABLE, COSTLY (viable with a named tax), or BLOCKED; the recommendation is the most central VIABLE tier unless a gate forces otherwise, and it carries the forcing gate's name.

**Synchronization physics.** Ring all-reduce time is split into a bandwidth term, 2(n−1)/n·S/B, which may hide behind compute up to an overlap fraction, and a latency term, 2(n−1) serially chained hops, which drains at the step boundary and is never hidden. Step efficiency is compute/(compute + exposed communication). PFC headroom is modeled as 2× the one-way BDP per port; InfiniBand's credit loop (12-bit FCCL, ~128 KB per virtual lane) yields ~1 km at 100 G by derivation.

**Fleet arithmetic.** A tower fleet (30,000 sites × 2 × L4 at 72 W, 242 TFLOPS dense FP8) is compared with a hub fleet (300 sites × 52 × H100 at 700 W within the 50 kW envelope) on schedulable FP8 per megawatt of site power and on how many of the twelve archetypes each rung can legally host.

## 3. Results

**The matrix.** (Figure: `figures/placement_matrix.png`.) Across twelve archetypes: training of any scale never leaves the central factory (fabric gate); RAN L1 baseband is forced to the hub by fronthaul, not by AI economics; the near-RT RIC loop is the *only* archetype latency forces below central — to the metro PoP, not the tower; video analytics and telemetry aggregation are forced edgeward by gravity (backhaul dollars), not milliseconds; sovereign inference pins to the metro PoP. A fantasy tower with a 1 MW feed stays blocked — the gates are orthogonal, and the suite asserts it.

**The fleet gap.** The tower fleet fields 60,000 GPUs on ~45 MW of provisioned envelope (~5.8 MW consumed); the hub fleet 15,600 GPUs on ~15 MW (~14.7 MW consumed) — and the hub fleet delivers ~6.4× more schedulable dense-FP8 per provisioned megawatt while legally hosting 10 of the 12 archetypes against the tower's 6. The accounting is stated openly: per *consumed* watt the L4 silicon is competitive (3.4 vs 2.8 TFLOPS/W); the tower fleet's cost is structural — a 2-device fabric quantum that strands ~87% of the envelope it makes the operator provision (the feed could power 15), against a hub that fills 52 of a 64-slot pod.

**Bandwidth cannot buy back distance.** (Figures: `figures/efficiency_cliff.png`, `figures/lossless_ceiling.png`.) With the latency term exposed, step efficiency collapses with distance at any bandwidth; quadrupling inter-site bandwidth moves the curve by <1%, consistent with the published ≤0.66% for doubling. The lossless ceiling renders the same wall in buffer megabytes: headroom demand crosses real switch buffers between metro and long-haul distances.

**The honest reversal.** Substituting a 30 km rural hub reverses one verdict: RAN L1 becomes tower-recommended, because the fronthaul budget (~20.4 km at 4.9 µs/km) can no longer reach the hub. The exception validates the cascade — the tower earns a GPU exactly where geography strands the radio.

## 4. Calibration and validation

Constants pin to published data points and the suite fails if they drift: Bifrost's measured 9.5 MB headroom (80 km, 100 G testbed) and simulated 286 MB (600 km, 400 G); Corning's ASTRA-sim behaviors (~26× at 1,000 km, ≤0.66% from doubling bandwidth, near-complete overlap below 10 km); the ~42 ms NY–SF RTT over ~4,148 km of route fiber at 4.9 µs/km; the ~20.4 km C-RAN radius from the ~100 µs one-way fronthaul budget; MetroX-3's 40 km ceiling and its absence from shipping AI clusters; GPU spec-sheet power/throughput for L4 and H100.

## 5. Related work

Cross-datacenter training studies (Corning's ASTRA-sim analysis; DiLoCo-style communication-reducing methods) quantify the distance penalty we gate on; Bifrost and deadlock-avoidance literature supply the lossless-headroom physics; O-RAN 7.2x and ITU-T timing profiles fix the fronthaul and synchronization budgets; AI-RAN industry material (AI-RAN Alliance, NVIDIA ARC) supplies the thesis under test. This work's contribution is the auditable cascade that turns those constraints into per-workload verdicts with named forcing gates, and the fleet-level consequence.

## 6. What a skeptic should attack

The tier envelopes (1.5 kW / 50 kW / 2 MW / 100 MW) are modeling assumptions, not a survey; the ring α–β model omits hierarchical collectives, pipeline parallelism, and communication-reducing training methods that *are* designed to buy back distance (DiLoCo-class methods change the exponent, not our gate structure, but a reader deploying them should re-derive); the twelve archetypes are archetypes; dense-FP8 spec throughput is not delivered throughput; the Corning study is a preprint and is simulation, not a production run; the fleet comparison charges provisioned power (per consumed watt the ordering inverts — both rows are printed) and prices neither real estate, backhaul, nor trenching. Every one of these is an exposed constant or profile in the code.

## 7. Conclusion

On the operator's own footprint, centralization is not a preference but the residual after the gates have spoken — and when they speak, they name fronthaul, gravity, and sovereignty, almost never milliseconds. The tower hosts a radio; the hub hosts the radio's compute; the metro PoP hosts the region's promises; the factory hosts the training. An operator who wants a different answer should not argue with the matrix — they should change an envelope and rerun it.
